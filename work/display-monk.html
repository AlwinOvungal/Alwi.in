Display Monk

Calm control for your displays. A free, open-source macOS menu bar app and command-line tool for every display setting macOS hides or leaves out: brightness and sync across screens, sharp HiDPI on any monitor, arrangement and monitor controls, in one quiet menu.

01 — The problem

macOS hides most display settings, or leaves them out.

Plug an external monitor into a Mac and most of its controls disappear. Brightness, contrast, volume and input live behind the monitor's own buttons. Apple silicon Macs don't offer sharp HiDPI (Retina) sizes for most monitors under 4K, so text looks soft. Arranging screens means a trip into System Settings.

The goal was one small, quiet menu that brings all of it back, works on every Mac from macOS 13, and stays out of the way the rest of the time.

02 — Design

Everything your displays need, and nothing they don't.

The details

  • Sync never fights you. Turning sync on keeps the display under the pointer as it is and matches the others to it. Changes made outside the app are never followed silently: the menu says Not matched and offers a Match button.
  • Down to black. The top three quarters of the slider drive the real backlight; the last quarter dims the image in software, all the way to black.
  • HiDPI, two ways. Instantly through a mirrored virtual screen, or made permanent in macOS's own display configuration, with no restart either way.
  • Keys follow you. Brightness keys control the display under the pointer; volume keys follow the selected sound output, with an on-screen display.

03 — Principles

Gentle on monitors and battery.

  • No polling. Idle means idle: about 0% CPU with the menu closed. Values are re-read only at launch, when displays change and when the menu opens.
  • Careful DDC. Repeated identical writes are skipped and writes are spaced at least 100 ms apart. Nothing is sent while displays sleep or for two seconds after they wake.
  • Reversible changes. A refresh rate change, a non-native size or a rotation must be confirmed within 15 seconds or it reverts.
  • Quietly honest. No account, no analytics. The update check is one anonymous request a day.

04 — Under the hood

DDC/CI
Hardware brightness, contrast, volume and input over I²C, on Apple silicon and Intel
Brightness
Native for Apple displays, DDC for monitors, gamma-table dimming for the rest
HiDPI
A HiDPI virtual display mirrored to the monitor, or a display override that needs no app
Keys
A system event tap for brightness and volume keys
Structure
A pure-Swift core with unit tests, a macOS display layer, the SwiftUI menu bar app and the CLI

05 — Shipping

One line to install.

Display Monk installs with a single Terminal command, or from a DMG. Releases are built by a GitHub Actions workflow. An in-app updater checks once a day, verifies the download's checksum and signature, replaces itself and relaunches.

curl -fsSL https://alwinovungal.github.io/Display-Monk/install.sh | bash
